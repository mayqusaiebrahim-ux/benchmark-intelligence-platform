# Executive Summary — Trip.com

**Benchmark:** TC-002 | **Date:** 2026-06-30 | **Overall:** 3.3 / 5.0 | **AI Maturity:** Assistive

---

## What is Trip.com doing today?

Trip.com is one of the world's largest OTAs (Nasdaq: TCOM, $20B+), operating a full-service booking platform across flights, hotels, trains, car rentals, and attractions. It serves 180+ countries with 1.4M+ hotels and 2M+ flights.

Its AI layer has two distinct products: **TripGenie** — a Q&A travel assistant that answers practical questions (disruption, visa, tips, jet lag) and provides inspiration — and **Trip.Planner** (labeled "New") — a structured form-to-map AI itinerary generator. Neither replaces the OTA booking engine; both enhance it.

Trip.com also auto-detects Arabic locale with full RTL interface — the deepest Arabic localization observed in this benchmark cycle.

---

## Why is this interaction valuable from a UX perspective?

**TripGenie solves a specific problem:** travelers have practical questions at every stage of their journey (before booking, during travel, at the airport), and they currently turn to Google, airline websites, or FAQs for answers. TripGenie consolidates this into the booking platform.

**Trip.Planner solves a different problem:** the blank-page problem of "I want to go to Japan for 7 days but don't know where to start." The structured form (From → To → Duration → Preferences) gives users guardrails without requiring them to type a perfect freeform prompt.

Together, they represent the **AI-as-Sidekick** model — AI that helps without replacing the product.

---

## What trend does it represent?

**The incumbent response to AI-first challengers.** Trip.com is showing how established OTAs are adding AI without rebuilding from scratch. TripGenie and Trip.Planner were added to a platform built for transactional booking. This is not AI-native — it is AI-enhanced.

This is the dominant industry model for the next 3–5 years. Mindtrip (AI-native, no booking) and Trip.com (full OTA + AI layer) represent the two poles. Most incumbents will follow the Trip.com path.

---

## How could this evolve in the next generation?

The gap in Trip.com's AI today: TripGenie is transactional (Q&A) and Trip.Planner is structured (form). The next evolution is when these two merge — a conversational AI that **both answers questions AND generates and books a full itinerary** without the user ever leaving the chat. When TripGenie can say "Here's your Japan itinerary — shall I book the flights and the Shinagawa hotel?" and complete the transaction, Trip.com will have overtaken Mindtrip on the metric that matters: conversion within the AI.

## What opportunities does this create for Saudia?

Three direct takeaways for Saudia, by timeline:

**Now (0–6 months):** Add "Ask Saudia" Q&A AI to Help & Support section, answering the 50 most common Saudia-specific questions (Hajj procedures, Alfursan redemption, onboard Islamic services, baggage rules by route). No trip planning required — just structured knowledge + LLM.

**Short-term (6–18 months):** Build a Saudia Trip.Planner equivalent. Pre-fill "Starting From" with the user's Saudia flight origin. Reduce the form to: Destination → Duration → Preferences. AI generates a map-integrated itinerary scoped to Saudia destinations.

**Medium-term (18–36 months):** Arabic-language AI that responds natively in Arabic with Gulf dialect awareness. Trip.com has Arabic UI — but no Arabic AI. Saudia can own this space.
