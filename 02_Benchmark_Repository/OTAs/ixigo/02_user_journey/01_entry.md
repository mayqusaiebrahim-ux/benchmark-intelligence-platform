## What I Saw

ixigo's homepage is a conventional multi-category OTA entry with a distinctive Indian market focus. The tab bar presents: Flights, Hotels, Trains, Buses, Cabs, Visa. The hero is dominated by a search form anchored to the selected mode.

Notable elements above generic:
- **ixigo Pro** — premium subscription tier promoted in the nav bar with a "Do More With ixigo Pro" CTA
- **Special Fares** — the flights form includes a "Special Fares" section: Student, Senior Citizen, Armed Forces discounts pre-selectable before search
- **Flight Tracker Free** — visible as a separate utility in the nav, indicating that flight status tracking is a standalone product
- **Credit Card** — ixigo AU Bank co-branded credit card in the navigation; fintech integration at the entry point
- **Plan** — a planning section in navigation (not a chatbot; appears to be an itinerary-adjacent feature)
- **Group Booking** — dedicated entry point in navigation
- **Fare Alerts** — mentioned in navigation (URL 404'd on web)

The page loads quickly. No AI interaction on first load. No geo-aware cold start observed. No conversational entry. Standard form-first OTA model.

## The 5 Questions

### 1. What is happening here?
A standard multi-modal OTA homepage. Form-based entry for all transport modes. The notable departures from the generic OTA format are: (1) fintech integration at nav level (credit card), (2) subscription tier visible before any interaction, (3) Special Fares segmentation at the search form level.

### 2. Why is this valuable from a UX perspective?
The Special Fares pre-selection (Student / Senior / Armed Forces) removes a step that most OTAs bury in a filter panel. By making it visible in the search form, ixigo signals that it understands its user demographics — and places the discount in the decision moment where it has most impact.

### 3. What trend does it represent?
**Segment-aware entry**: OTAs recognizing that different traveler types have different search contexts and surfacing that segmentation at the entry point rather than filtering it. This is a mild form of the broader personalization trend without requiring any data or login.

### 4. How could this evolve?
When combined with login and Alfursan-style loyalty data, segment-aware entry becomes: "Welcome back. I've pre-selected Business Class and your usual travel companions." The entry point becomes the personalization moment.

### 5. What opportunity does this create for Saudia?
Add a segment selector to the flight search form: Business / Economy / Pilgrim (Hajj/Umrah). Each segment changes the default search context, the fare type shown, and the ancillary upsell priority. Pilgrim segment could unlock the Hajj AI companion. This is implementable as a UX change with minimal backend work.
