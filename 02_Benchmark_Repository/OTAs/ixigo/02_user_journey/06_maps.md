# 06 Maps — ixigo
**Step Score:** 2.8 / 5.0 | **Innovation Score:** 2.5 / 5.0

---

## What I Saw

No meaningful map interface was found on ixigo web. The product does not use maps as a primary interface for discovery, search, or navigation. This contrasts sharply with Mindtrip (AI-filtered map as a core interaction model) and Trip.com (Trip.Planner with map-based itinerary output).

The **Coach Position Predictor** and **Platform Locator** are arguably spatial tools — they tell you where to be physically — but they are represented as text/visual diagrams, not on a map.

---

## The 5 Questions

### 1. What is happening here?
Maps are not part of ixigo's UX vocabulary. This is appropriate for a rail-first OTA: train journeys are point-to-point, station-to-station. Maps matter more for destination exploration and hotel proximity, where ixigo has not invested on web.

### 2. Why does this matter?
As ixigo expands into hotels and destination experiences, the absence of a map layer will become a gap vs. OTAs that integrate maps into search results.

### 3. What trend does it represent?
The spatial UX gap in rail-native OTAs: the map is not the interface when the journey is a fixed rail corridor. This will change as ixigo expands into activities and destination experiences where spatial context matters.

### 4. How could this evolve?
A "station neighborhood map": when a user books a train to a city, ixigo shows a map of the station area with AI-recommended hotels, restaurants, and transport options within walking distance of the platform exit. The train arrival becomes a geo-anchor for local discovery.

### 5. What opportunity does this create for Saudia?
**Airport micro-map**: Saudia could launch an AI-powered airport map layer within its app showing the passenger's current terminal position, gate, lounge access points, and recommended restaurants within their time window before boarding. This directly analogizes ixigo's Coach Position Predictor idea but for airports, where Saudia owns the data.
