## What I Saw

Most ancillary URLs returned 404 on web (insurance, visa, fare calendar, holiday packages). However, two observed features are genuinely innovative:

### Order Food On Train
Accessible from the Trains quick-access strip. ixigo enables IRCTC's e-catering service — passengers can order meals from restaurant partners and have food delivered to their seat at selected stations mid-journey.

This is **AI at the point of consumption**, not at the point of planning. The passenger is already on the train, already in their seat, and the AI serves them contextual options based on their booking (which train, which seat, which upcoming stations accept delivery).

### Airport Cabs
ixigo Cabs: "Airport Cabs — On-Time Pick-up — No Last Minute Cancellation." Last-mile transport integrated at the point of booking. Not AI-driven, but contextually relevant.

### Fare Alerts (mentioned, not captured)
Fare alerts appear in navigation but URL returned 404. This is a classic ancillary that would be AI-driven in a mature implementation.

### What's Missing
- No travel insurance AI
- No seat selection AI (beyond the IRCTC quota system)
- No visa AI (URL 404)
- No hotel upsell at train booking moment
- No activity or experience layer

## The 5 Questions

### 1. What is happening here?
ixigo's ancillary coverage is limited to what the app exposes on web. Order Food On Train is the standout — it is uniquely positioned as an in-journey AI service layer, not a pre-departure add-on.

### 2. Why is this valuable from a UX perspective?
Order Food On Train removes the anxiety of "will there be good food at the station." By pre-ordering, the passenger has control over their in-journey nutrition without relying on station vendors. It converts an anxious moment into a confirmed service — the same psychology as airport lounge booking.

### 3. What trend does it represent?
**Journey-layer AI**: AI services delivered at specific points within the journey itself, not just before or after. This is the precursor to the in-flight AI assistant concept — AI that is present at the moment of need, not just at booking. This is nascent in travel globally. ixigo's implementation is train-specific but conceptually ahead of most airline apps.

### 4. How could this evolve?
In-journey AI concierge: "You'll be at Pune station in 45 minutes. Based on your dietary preferences, I've pre-selected 3 meal options from Honest Restaurant. Want me to confirm your order? You have 20 minutes." This turns a passive feature into a proactive AI action.

### 5. What opportunity does this create for Saudia?
**In-flight meal pre-selection AI**: Saudia already has in-flight meal options. ixigo's "Order Food On Train" model suggests the next evolution: AI recommends a meal based on the passenger's dietary preferences (derived from past flights, Alfursan profile, or a quick preference question at check-in). The meal is pre-confirmed before boarding. No menu anxiety on the flight. This is implementable through Saudia's existing IFRS (In-Flight Retail System) with an AI recommendation layer.
